Copywriting controls Draft

Tone of voice is only the surface. I map how your writing carries meaning (semantics) and what it does for the reader (pragmatics), and I set rules for syntax, because how sentences are built often matters most. Tone dials such as warmth, authority, brevity, energy and formality sit alongside them.

Within those rules sit writing mechanisms for the jobs your copy has to do: explaining complex ideas clearly and holding energy without shouting. For brands whose value rests on authorship and standards, a luxury, authority-led posture is available as an optional layer, encounter positioning. It writes as if the right reader already recognises your standard. It declares instead of pleads, chooses what to leave unsaid, and offers a designed point of access in place of sales pressure.

Each place your writing appears has its own register. A press advertisement, a LinkedIn article and a TikTok caption don’t sound the same, and they shouldn’t. The files describe how your brand speaks on each surface.

Image mapping, where imagery matters Draft

A style can be described without showing a single image. I describe how your imagery behaves, not what it shows: the texture of a surface, the energy of a line, the quality of light, and how much room the important things are given to breathe. Each becomes a setting a tool can follow.

I also build a LUT (look-up table) file for your imagery. It maps lighting and camera lens choices, among other mechanisms, to numerical controls, so you can fine-tune the look of your images with numbers instead of guesswork.

Your image files stay where they are. The standard describes your imagery in plain language and can point to files when a workflow needs them. The image side of the standard is still being defined.

Good to know

Commitments and limits

What I commit to

  • Your files are plain text (Markdown and JSON in a folder), so any AI tool that reads natural language can use them.
  • They’re portable. They’re easy to move, and easy to edit for different tools and workflows.
  • The structure follows the open BRND Standard, and is built around ideas AI tools already work with, such as voice, tone, positioning and visual logic.
  • You keep your files, and you decide what you share.

What I can’t promise

  • Identical results from every AI tool. Different tools read the same files slightly differently, and I don’t control what AI companies do.

A standard that keeps moving

The standard is never finished. Each version is a point in time, because the sands keep moving. The foundations are in place, and that matters more. Your business will change, and your files can change with it.

The public version on GitHub is 0.1 alpha, and version 0.2 is nearly ready. The version I build with has been through a year of testing and is further along. Draft
